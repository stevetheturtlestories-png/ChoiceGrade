How to compare electrical panel upgrade quotes

Two electricians can both say “panel upgrade” and price very different jobs. Before choosing a bid, find out exactly what is being replaced, whether your home's available electrical capacity changes, and who handles the utility, permit and inspection.

Use the quote checklistCopy questions for bidders
Start with one question

Is this a replacement panel, an increase to the electrical service supplying the house, a subpanel, or a combination? A panel labeled “200 amp” does not by itself mean the utility service and its conductors have been upgraded to 200 amps. Ask the electrician to describe the complete proposed system in writing.

First, make sure you're pricing the same job

A panel replacement changes the breaker box and moves or reconnects circuits as specified. A service upgrade can involve equipment between the utility connection and the panel, and may require work or approval from the utility. A subpanel provides additional circuit locations but does not automatically add capacity to the service. A quote may cover more than one of these.

Tell each electrician what you want to accomplish: replacing a damaged or obsolete panel, adding an EV charger, installing a heat pump, or making room for future circuits. Ask what assessment supports their recommendation, including a load calculation when additional demand is proposed. ENERGY STAR's electric-ready guidance explains why new equipment can affect capacity and why a panel change can sometimes lead to additional service work. The right solution depends on your home and local requirements; a larger service is not automatically necessary for every project.

Ten things to compare in electrical quotes

Mark each item included, excluded or unclear in every proposal. “Unclear” means you need a written answer, not that the contractor plans to omit the work.

CompareAsk for this in writing
1. Reason and capacityWhat problem is being solved? What is the existing service capacity, what is proposed, and what load assessment supports the choice? Are future EV charging or electric appliances included in the assumptions?
2. Panel or serviceIs this panel replacement only, a service upgrade, a subpanel or a combination? State the proposed panel rating and the actual service capacity separately.
3. EquipmentSpecify panel brand, model or series, number of usable circuit positions, new breakers and any surge protection. Say which existing breakers are reused, if permitted by the proposed equipment and local rules.
4. Work outside the panelIdentify whether the meter socket or base, service conductors, mast or weatherhead, grounding and bonding work, and utility-side changes are included, excluded or dependent on inspection.
5. Existing circuitsHow many circuits will be moved? Are new circuits for an EV charger or other appliances part of the bid? Will circuits be identified and the finished panel directory labeled?
6. Site and repairsWill the panel be relocated? Who handles drywall, paint, exterior siding, trenching or other repairs if needed? State any assumptions about access to walls or buried wiring.
7. Utility and outageWho contacts the electric utility and schedules any disconnect and reconnect? How long is the expected outage, and what happens if utility work changes the schedule or price?
8. Permit and inspectionWho obtains and pays for required permits, schedules inspection, corrects deficiencies related to their work and gives you the final approval record? Requirements vary by city, county and state.
9. Price and changesSeparate included work, allowances, permit or utility fees, optional work and exclusions. What conditions could add cost, and must you approve a written change before work continues?
10. Timing and warrantyGet the expected start date, time without power, completion target, equipment warranty, labor warranty and who will handle a problem after installation.

For exact protective devices and installation methods, ask the electrician to identify what your local code and inspector require. This guide does not specify how electrical work should be performed.

Where two quotes can look comparable but aren't

Both say “200 amp panel”

One bidder may be replacing the panel on the existing service. The other may be proposing new service equipment and utility coordination to increase available capacity. Ask both for the existing and proposed service capacity, what equipment between the utility connection and panel changes, and what utility work is assumed. Compare prices only after the scopes match.

The EV charger quote includes a “panel upgrade”

Ask what load assessment shows the need for it and whether a suitable alternative is available for your situation. Then check whether the charger, its circuit, the wiring route, wall repairs and any utility work are included. A price for the box alone will not cover the same job as a complete charger installation.

The permit is “by owner”

Ask what that means where you live, who is legally allowed to obtain the permit, who books the inspection, and who is responsible for corrections. Confirm the arrangement with your local building department before signing. A permit fee excluded from one bid can change the apparent price difference.

These are illustrative comparisons, not actual customer projects or estimates of typical prices.

Send the same questions to every electrician

Subject: Questions about my electrical panel quote

Hi [name], thanks for your proposal. Before I decide, could you please confirm:

  1. Our existing and proposed electrical service capacities, whether this is a panel replacement or service upgrade, and the assessment behind your recommendation.
  2. The panel brand or model, circuit positions, included breakers and any new circuits.
  3. Whether meter equipment, service wiring, grounding and bonding, and any utility changes are included.
  4. Who contacts the utility, obtains the permit, schedules the inspection and provides the final approval record.
  5. The expected power outage and any wall, paint, siding or other repairs included in your price.
  6. Which items are excluded or could add cost, and how I would approve changes.
  7. The proposed start and completion dates, payment schedule, and equipment and labor warranties.

Could you put these answers in the revised written quote? Thank you, [your name]

Before you sign

  • Check the electrician's required license or registration and insurance with your state or local authority, and confirm who will perform the work.
  • Compare the revised written scopes, total cost, exclusions, payment terms and responsibilities after receiving answers.
  • Keep the quote, approved changes, permit and inspection records, final panel labeling and warranty documents.
  • If a bidder says work can proceed without a permit, verify that directly with the local authority. Permit requirements depend on where the home is located and the work proposed.
